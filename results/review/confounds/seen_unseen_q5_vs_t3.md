# Confound: held-out vs seen; per-constraint by k; Q5 vs T3 (agent report)

Held-out vs seen: no gap anywhere (12/13 comparisons held-out >= seen; the only p<=0.05, Qwen3.5 T3 pairs 0.60 vs 0.47, favours held-out). Caveat: Q5's two 5-combos are supersets of every evaluated triple, so "held_out" is not a hold-out for Q5; S1 never saw triples; T3 is the only true hold-out at k=3 (gap +0.015 Qwen3-8B, +0.011 Qwen3.5).
Per-constraint by k (Qwen3-8B, pooled per-constraint binary): S1 .42/.44/.65, T3 .52/.56/.69, Q5 .43/.54/.65, base .11/.15/.15. Rises with k in EVERY trained arm incl. S1 -> a property of the prompt/inference regime (more rules in context -> stricter formatting), not of multi-constraint training. Driven by no_comma, end_checker, capital (and end_of_sentence in T3/Q5); reasoning_language at ceiling; number_words flat or falling.
Q5 vs T3 (Qwen3-8B, joint): k=1 .425 vs .517, McNemar p=.035 (17 vs 6 discordant; robustness p=.064); k=3 .256 vs .245 (p=.84); k=5 .410 vs .308 (p=.22, n=39). Per-constraint pooled T3 >= Q5 at every k; Q5's deficit in number_words and end_checker. Q5 == S1 on singles (p=1.0); T3 > S1 (p=.004).
Finding: "monotonically better with more constraints per example" rejected; consistent with "diversity of combinations matters" but k and number of combinations are confounded across arms (S1 6 combos, T3 12, Q5 2). Q5 had 2-6x more per-constraint exposure than T3 yet lower per-constraint compliance at every k.
Implication: for a fixed budget sample many distinct combinations at moderate k; decisive test = k=5 from many combinations (needs a larger constraint set) or a mixed arm with all combos.
Confidence: moderate-high on no held-out gap and rises-with-k in all arms; moderate on Q5 < T3 singles (one uncorrected paired test); not supported: Q5 > T3 at k=5.
